# Skill · Will · Fit · Capacity
### A Monthly 1:1 Framework for People Leaders

---

## The Four Dimensions

| Dimension | Core Question |
|-----------|--------------|
| ⚡ **Skill** | Does this person have the competencies, knowledge, and technical ability to do the job well? |
| 🔥 **Will** | Does this person have the motivation, drive, and desire to perform and grow? |
| 🎯 **Fit** | Is this person in the right role for their strengths, values, and working style? |
| ⏱ **Capacity** | Does this person have the time, energy, and bandwidth to perform at their best? |

---

## How to Use This Guide

This framework gives you a structured lens for your monthly 1:1s. Rather than reviewing all four dimensions every month, each quarter focuses on one or two dimensions in depth — while keeping a light pulse on the others. Over time, you build a rich picture of each person's development, blockers, and potential.

The questions are prompts, not a checklist. Use them to open a conversation, not to interview. Your job is to listen, challenge, and support — not to score.

> **Monthly rhythm:** Each 1:1 follows the same structure — Review → Current focus → Grandmother Rule → Action points.
>
> **Grandmother Rule:** Start with 2 positives, then 1 challenge. Keep it concrete and specific.
>
> **Action points:** Always close with at least one thing you or they will do before next time.
>
> **Trust first:** The framework only works if the person feels safe being honest. Build psychological safety before going deep.

## Quarter 1 — Establishing the Baseline

Before you can coach effectively, you need to understand where each person actually stands. The first three months are about building that picture — across all four dimensions — without jumping to conclusions or solutions.

---

### Month 1: Skill — Mapping Current Competence

**Introduction**

This is the first month of using this framework. Before anything else, you need to understand what this person can actually do — not just on paper, but in practice. Resist the urge to assess too quickly. Your job this month is to ask and listen.

**Review from Last Month**

N/A — this is the starting point. Revisit in month 2.

**Monthly Focus: Skill Assessment**

The core question: *Does this person have the skills needed to succeed in their current role and grow toward their next?*

Conversation starters for Skill:
- What parts of your work do you feel most confident in right now?
- Which areas do you find most challenging — technically or otherwise?
- If you had to pick one skill you want to be significantly stronger in 6 months from now, what would it be?
- What's the biggest skill gap you see between where you are and where you want to go?

Your job after this conversation:
- Note the gap between their self-assessment and your own observation.
- Identify 1–2 concrete skills to develop this quarter — together with the employee.
- Begin building an informal skill map for your team.

**Assessment:** ⚡ Skill — `Low / Medium / High`

> **Tip:** Self-assessment and manager assessment rarely match perfectly. That gap is valuable data — explore it with curiosity, not judgment.

**Closing Points**
- Don't overwhelm people with a list of development areas in month one. One thing done well beats five things half-started.
- Some people overestimate their skills (Dunning-Kruger), others underestimate them (impostor syndrome). Your calibration matters.
- If you have challenges or just want to talk something through — don't wait until next month's 1:1. I always have 20 minutes.

### Month 2: Will — Understanding Motivation

**Introduction**

Now that you have a sense of the person's skill level, it's time to understand what drives them. Will is about motivation, energy, and desire — not just for their current tasks, but for their career and role overall. People with high skill but low will are often a sign that something is wrong in the environment or role match.

**Review from Last Month**
- Skill assessment from Month 1 — what did you learn? What are you following up on?
- Grandmother Rule: 2 positives, 1 challenge.

**Monthly Focus: Will Assessment**

The core question: *Does this person want to do this work — and does the environment allow them to bring their best?*

Conversation starters for Will:
- What kind of work energizes you most right now?
- What kinds of tasks drain you — that you push off or dread?
- When do you feel most motivated on the team? What makes those moments different?
- Is there something you wish you were working on that you're not?
- What would make you more motivated in your current role?

Your job after this conversation:
- Distinguish between low will due to role misfit versus low will due to context (bad sprint, personal issues, unclear goals).
- Identify one thing you can change in how work is structured or assigned to lift their motivation.
- Note: sustained low will without clear reason is a signal — don't ignore it.

**Assessment:** 🔥 Will — `Low / Medium / High`

> **Warning sign:** High skill + low will is often more critical than low skill + high will. Unmotivated experts disengage silently.
>
> Motivation is context-dependent. Ask whether it's the task, the team, the direction, or something outside work.

**Closing Points**
- People don't always know why they are demotivated. Give them time and space to reflect before the meeting.
- If someone is clearly struggling with motivation, don't jump to performance management. First ask: what changed?
- If you have challenges or just want to talk something through — don't wait until next month's 1:1. I always have 20 minutes.

---

### Month 3: Fit — Role and Culture Alignment

**Introduction**

After mapping skill and will, it's time to ask a deeper question: Is this person in the right role? Fit is about whether the job matches who they are — their values, working style, strengths, and ambitions. Great people in the wrong role underperform. It's not their fault.

**Review from Last Month**
- Will conversation from Month 2 — follow-up on motivation patterns.
- Grandmother Rule: 2 positives, 1 challenge.

**Monthly Focus: Fit Assessment**

The core question: *Is this role a natural fit for how this person thinks, works, and what they value?*

Conversation starters for Fit:
- Does your role feel like a good match for how you like to work — your pace, your style, your strengths?
- Is there a part of your job that feels like it's fighting your natural instincts?
- If you designed your ideal version of this role, what would look different?
- How aligned do you feel with the direction the team and company is heading?
- Do you feel like you belong here — that your values match what we do and how we do it?

Your job after this conversation:
- Separate role fit from culture fit — they can diverge.
- If there is a fit issue, explore: can the role be shaped, or is a different path better for them?
- Avoid the instinct to dismiss fit concerns as "just complaining" — they are often early warning signals.

**Assessment:** 🎯 Fit — `Low / Medium / High`

> **Role fit** can often be improved — adjust responsibilities, remove tasks, add ownership.
>
> **Culture fit** is harder to change. If someone's values genuinely clash with the team's, be honest about it early.

**Closing Points**
- Fit conversations can feel existential for people. Make clear that exploring fit is not a threat — it's an investment in them.
- Sometimes the best outcome for a person is a role change. That can be a leadership success, not a failure.
- If you have challenges or just want to talk something through — don't wait until next month's 1:1. I always have 20 minutes.

## Quarter 2 — Going Deeper

You now have a baseline picture across Skill, Will, and Fit. Quarter 2 is about acting on what you learned — while introducing the fourth dimension: Capacity. You will also revisit earlier findings to see what has changed.

---

### Month 4: Capacity — Bandwidth and Sustainability

**Introduction**

Capacity is the most overlooked dimension. A person can have the skill, the will, and the fit — and still underperform because they are running at 110% with no breathing room. High performers often hide capacity problems until it's too late. Your job is to create a space where they can be honest about this.

**Review from Last Month**
- Grandmother Rule: 2 positives, 1 challenge.
- Quick pulse on Skill, Will, Fit — any changes since last quarter?

**Monthly Focus: Capacity Assessment**

The core question: *Does this person have enough time, mental energy, and focus to do their best work — sustainably?*

Conversation starters for Capacity:
- How does your workload feel right now — manageable, stretched, or over the edge?
- Are there things on your plate that you feel shouldn't be there, or that could be handled differently?
- When did you last have a week where you felt in control of your work — not just reacting?
- Are meetings, admin, or interruptions eating into your ability to do focused work?
- How is your energy level outside of work? Are you recovering properly?

Your job after this conversation:
- Distinguish between short-term peak load (expected) and structural overload (a problem).
- Look for commitments that can be removed, delegated, or restructured.
- Track whether capacity issues repeat — chronic overload is a systemic issue, not a personal one.

**Assessment:** ⏱ Capacity — `Low / Medium / High`

> **High performers often say "I'm fine" longest.** Watch for behavioral signals: shorter replies, missed details, low energy.
>
> Capacity problems often come from the top. Ask yourself: what have I contributed to this person's overload?

**Closing Points**
- People often feel they should handle full load without complaint. Normalize the conversation before you start it.
- Fixing capacity problems isn't soft — it directly impacts quality, retention, and your team's output.
- If you have challenges or just want to talk something through — don't wait until next month's 1:1. I always have 20 minutes.

### Month 5: Development Plan — Connecting the Four Dimensions

**Introduction**

You now have a picture across all four dimensions. Month 5 is about turning that picture into action. Together with the person, create a focused development plan — one that addresses the real levers, not just the most visible ones. A development plan that ignores Will or Capacity will fail.

**Review from Last Month**
- Capacity follow-up from Month 4.
- Grandmother Rule: 2 positives, 1 challenge.

**Monthly Focus: Co-creating the Development Plan**

The core question: *What 1–3 things, if improved or changed, would have the most impact on this person's performance and satisfaction?*

For each dimension, agree on a status and one action:

| Dimension | Action Question |
|-----------|----------------|
| ⚡ **Skill** | What is the most valuable skill to develop in the next quarter? How will we do it? |
| 🔥 **Will** | Is there something we can change about how or what they work on to raise motivation? |
| 🎯 **Fit** | Are there role adjustments that would make this a better match for their strengths? |
| ⏱ **Capacity** | What can be removed, delegated, or restructured to protect their focus? |

The development plan should be:
- **Written down** — even if short. A shared document creates accountability.
- **Owned by them** — you support, they drive.
- **Reviewed monthly** — not filed and forgotten.
- **Realistic** — 1–2 focus areas max. More than that and nothing gets done.

> A good development plan addresses the real constraint — not just the most visible one.
>
> If someone has low Will, a skills course won't fix it. Solve the root cause first.

**Closing Points**
- Development plans only work if the person believes in them. Don't create a plan for someone — create it with them.
- Revisit the plan briefly every month. Small adjustments keep it alive. Ignoring it kills it.
- If you have challenges or just want to talk something through — don't wait until next month's 1:1. I always have 20 minutes.

### Month 6: Reflection — What the Model Has Revealed

**Introduction**

Six months in. You have now used the Skill-Will-Fit-Capacity model long enough to see patterns. This month is about stepping back — reflecting on what you have learned about each person and about yourself as a coach and leader.

**Review from Last Month**
- Development plan follow-up from Month 5.
- Grandmother Rule: 2 positives, 1 challenge.

**Monthly Focus: Six-Month Reflection**

Questions for the person:
- Looking back at the last six months — what has changed for you? In your work, your confidence, your role?
- Is there a dimension — Skill, Will, Fit, or Capacity — where you feel you have grown the most?
- Where do you still feel stuck or uncertain?
- Has our 1:1 rhythm been useful? What would make it even better?

Questions for yourself (as leader):
- Have I been too focused on one dimension and neglected the others?
- Where have I been too slow to act on what I saw?
- Have I created enough psychological safety for honest conversations?
- Which of my people am I most uncertain about — and why?

Set direction for the next six months:
- Update the development plan based on what you have learned.
- Identify whether any dimension has shifted significantly — and what that means.
- Agree on one meaningful goal for the next quarter.

> The model is a tool, not a label. Avoid thinking of people as "high Skill / low Will" — people change, and so do contexts.
>
> Your role as a leader is not to fix people. It is to create the conditions where they can fix things themselves.

**Closing Points**
- The best 1:1 leaders are not the ones who give the best advice. They are the ones who ask the best questions.
- Leadership is not about closing gaps — it is about helping people find and operate from their strengths.
- After six months, you know your people well. Use that knowledge generously.

## Quick Reference — The Four Dimensions

| Dimension | What it covers | Key levers |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| ⚡ **Skill** | Competence, knowledge, technical ability, domain expertise | Practice, training, mentoring, stretch assignments |
| 🔥 **Will** | Motivation, drive, desire to perform and grow | Recognition, autonomy, purpose, team dynamics, role clarity |
| 🎯 **Fit** | Alignment between person, role, team, and company culture | Values, working style, career direction |
| ⏱ **Capacity** | Available bandwidth, mental energy, sustainable workload | Commitments, meetings, personal circumstances, systems |

---

## Common Patterns and What They Signal

| Pattern | What it often means |
|---------|---------------------|
| High Skill, Low Will | Role misfit, unclear purpose, burnout, lack of challenge or recognition |
| High Will, Low Skill | Over-promoted or new to role. Invest in deliberate skill development now |
| High Skill + Will, Low Capacity | Structural overload. Remove work before adding development |
| Low Fit, everything else OK | Good employee in wrong role or team. Explore a move before it becomes an exit |
| All four high | A star in the right place. Keep them challenged and visible |
| All four low | Critical situation. Act fast — understand root cause before labeling it a performance issue |

---

> **Remember:** The model diagnoses — it does not prescribe. The right action always depends on the person, the context, and your relationship.
>
> Use these conversations to build trust over time. The more trust, the more honest the answers — and the more useful the model becomes.
